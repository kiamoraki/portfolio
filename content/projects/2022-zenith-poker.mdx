  <p><a href="https://zenithpoker.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Zenith Poker</a> is an online poker training platform & community providing both free and paid content.</p>

<Text>
  <h2>Why a Redesign?</h2>
  <p><strong>Identity.</strong> The previous site was an out-of-the-box WordPress theme with a jarring complementary color scheme. Complementary colors sit on opposite sides of the color wheel; matched together, they create high-vibrational contrast. Used gently, they make content pop; used bluntly, they cancel each other out and muddy the visual landscape.</p>
  <p><strong>Imagery.</strong> The WordPress theme called for images to link to the course content. Trying to match generic stock imagery to abstract concepts led to unrelated pairings, devaluing the rich content inside.</p>
  <p><strong>Nomenclature.</strong> Inconsistent, opaque naming conventions created cognitive disconnect — bucketing postflop offerings as "library" and preflop offerings as "preflop." Generic terminology for very specific content creates confusion and adds extra mental effort.</p>
  <p><strong>Nests.</strong> The course hierarchy is organized in four tiers: Tracks &gt; Courses &gt; Lessons &gt; Topics. Communicating where you are in the hierarchy was being achieved through an accordion requiring multiple clicks. My challenge was to balance relationship wayfinding with ease of content access.</p>
  <h2>Objectives</h2>
  <p><strong>Increase Registrations.</strong> Emphasize free content as a means to encourage registration.</p>
  <p><strong>Highlight Discord Community.</strong> With over 2,700 members, a lot of purchases are driven via Discord dialogue.</p>
  <p><strong>Flow.</strong> Create a fluid journey from sign-up to course access. For pre-existing users, create a content hub to access purchases and track progress.</p>
</Text>

<Text>
  <h2>Identity</h2>
  <p>I imagine the Zenith Student navigating the statistical poker matrix, stacking towers of chips in golden art-deco metaverse rooms. Drawing inspiration from the felt green of poker tables, the recognizable symbols of spade, heart, club, and diamond, and the intricate patterns adorning the back of playing cards.</p>
</Text>

<Figure src="/img/221117-zenith_poker/design-inspo.png" />

<Text>
  <h2>Competitor Analysis</h2>
  <p>Before jumping into design, I surveyed other poker training sites. 50% used a dark theme, and most used red accent colors. The dark themes aligned with the spaces I conjure when thinking about poker — dark smoky rooms, tuxedos, and bourbon.</p>
</Text>
<Figure src="/img/221117-zenith_poker/competitors.png" />

<Text>
  <h2>Imagery</h2>
  <p>I could have scoured stock photo sites, but the abstract concepts proved difficult to match coherently. Custom illustrations were beyond scope and would inhibit future course additions. I opted to go with a text-forward, button-like appearance.</p>
  <h2>Design System</h2>
  <h3>Palette</h3>
  <p>I chose green as the primary color as a nod to felt-topped IRL poker tables. The secondary color is a vibrant blue similar to Discord's purple — a subtle way to strengthen the tie between the site and the community.</p>
</Text>
<Figure src="/img/221117-zenith_poker/palette.png" />

<Text>
  <h3>Typography</h3>
  <p>Keeping with the "living in the machine" theme, I chose fonts that felt futuristic, sans-serif, blocky, and minimalistic.</p>
</Text>

<Text>
  <h2>Homepage Design Evolution</h2>
  <p>Illustrating the iterative process — a surprising journey from inspiration to final product. We move from broad strokes to refined pixel pushing. I love to include clients in this process; for people inexperienced with the flow, it can be challenging to be constructive in the early phases. That first pass is like the first time you put on makeup and come out looking like a clown — too much of everything. Incremental additive movements focused on identifying what is working and building upon it.</p>
</Text>
