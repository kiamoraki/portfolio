description: |
  Roses is based on the mathematical Rose Curve algorithm, a simple equation capable of generating a range of geometric formations through small parametric adjustments.

  The project emerged from an interest in variation within constraint. Rather than focusing on a single image, I became interested in the entire family of forms generated by the algorithm. Each configuration represents a unique expression of the same mathematical architecture, demonstrating how diversity can emerge from a common set of rules. The work investigates the relationship between structure and possibility, asking how small shifts within a system can produce radically different outcomes.

  Multiple animated variations were layered together to create a dense, evolving visual environment. As the forms intersect and overlap, individual patterns become part of a larger field of movement and complexity. The resulting composition oscillates between legibility and abstraction, allowing viewers to experience both the underlying order of the system and the unexpected forms it generates.

  The work was exhibited in _The Portal_, an 18-screen video installation constructed off-grid in the desert for Mars College. Within this setting, the piece functioned as both a visualization of mathematical emergence and a reflection on collective systems. Just as a simple algorithm can generate countless unique configurations, communities, cultures, and ecosystems evolve through variations within shared structures. Roses explores this generative principle as a bridge between mathematics, aesthetics, and social organization.
